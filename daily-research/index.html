Global / rotavirus Abstract cross-checked

Rotavirus vaccination and gastroenteritis mortality

Pooled test-negative case-control studies across 24 countries. The mortality subset covered 16 countries and 21,522 children, with 183 gastroenteritis deaths and 25 rotavirus-positive deaths. Among eligible children at least 3 months old who had received other routine vaccines, adjusted effectiveness of at least one rotavirus dose was 75.8% (95% CI 28.4–91.8) against rotavirus-positive gastroenteritis mortality, based on an analysis population of 13,630. All-cause gastroenteritis mortality estimate was statistically inconclusive. This does not compare fully unvaccinated versus vaccinated populations.

Dataset correction: Correction to Oct 10 chat summary: 24 countries in pooled dataset, 16 countries with mortality events. Do not use 27,252 or 22 as the mortality-study denominator.

Maternal vaccination / RSV Abstract cross-checked

Maternal RSV immunization: clinical protection

An October 2026 systematic review found vaccine efficacy of 84% against severe RSV-related lower respiratory tract infection in infants (two randomized trials, 7,656 participants). This is an outcome-specific estimate, not an all-product pooled safety guarantee. Trial protocols, pregnancy timing and formulations remain relevant.

Manufacturer safety / RSV Original trial cross-checked

GSK investigational maternal RSV product: preterm-birth signal

In a manufacturer-funded, placebo-controlled trial of GSK's RSVPreF3-Mat candidate, preterm births occurred in 237/3,494 vaccine-group infants (6.8%) versus 86/1,739 placebo infants (4.9%); relative risk 1.37 (95% CI 1.08–1.74). Enrollment stopped early. This GSK candidate is NOT Pfizer's licensed Abrysvo and findings must not be merged across products.

Primary source ↗
NEJM trial, 2024
Breastfeeding / integrative Original trials cross-checked

Breastfeeding around oral rotavirus vaccination

Two earlier randomized studies (each enrolled 400 infant/mother pairs) found no improvement in vaccine IgA response from withholding breastfeeding. Pakistan: 28.2% versus 37.8% seroconversion after three doses, with withholding versus immediate feeding. North India: 26% versus 27%. These assess immune markers, not severe disease, and do not support withholding feeds.

Herbal safety / integrative Abstract cross-checked

Heavy-metal contamination of herbal products

September 14, 2026 review of 70 studies from 27 countries/territories found a pooled lead concentration around 1.93 mg/kg in herbal materials/products, with substantial study heterogeneity. Product concentration does not equal an infant's absorbed dose. There is no clinical evidence here that herbal use clears vaccine constituents.

Reviews on Environmental Health, Sept 14, 2026

India / ROTASIIL Original article cross-checked

ROTASIIL and rotavirus hospitalization

An observational test-negative study across 32 hospitals in seven Indian states examined 2,243 hospitalized children aged 3–23 months, including 638 rotavirus-positive children. Estimated adjusted effectiveness of three ROTASIIL doses against rotavirus gastroenteritis hospitalization was 52% (95% CI 32–66%). Nonrandomized design, with potential residual confounding.

Primary source ↗
Nature Communications, Sept 15, 2026
China / manufacturing Original article cross-checked

Rotarix PCV-free manufacturing comparison

Randomized observer-blinded trial in 2,000 Chinese infants comparing PCV-free Rotarix with the original Rotarix. Seroconversion criterion met noninferiority, but antibody geometric mean concentration ratio did not meet its separate prespecified criterion. Serious adverse events were 26.1% versus 25.2%, with none considered vaccine-related by investigators. Immunogenicity is not itself a clinical disease endpoint.

Primary source ↗
Human Vaccines & Immunotherapeutics, 2026
TCM processing / integrative Original review cross-checked

Traditional Chinese medicine processing and measured chemical transformation

A June 2026 systematic review describes how processing toxic materials such as Aconitum can hydrolyze toxic diterpenoid alkaloids, including aconitine. This is evidence about reducing toxicity OF medicinal materials during preparation, not about administering herbs to accelerate elimination of vaccines or pharmaceuticals from infants.

Primary source ↗
Chinese Medicine, June 10, 2026
